# DELTA-V: DOMINION — Campaign Bible

> **Bring your people home.** Dominion is a carrier-based, combined-arms campaign: you can fight from any
> seat (boots, rover, boat, VTOL, the Kestrel's guns), and the people who come back to the Kestrel matter.
> You are capable, never superhuman. A mission can be a tactical success and still hurt; an orderly
> withdrawal that saves everyone can be the best thing you do all war.
>
> Touchstones (studied, never copied): the carrier hub and deployment rhythm of *Infinite Warfare*, the
> air-sea-ground interdependence of *Carrier Command 2* and *Battlefield 4: Naval Strike*, the human-scale
> staging of *Halo: Reach/ODST*, the pilot-and-machine bond of *Titanfall 2*, the readable call-ins of
> *Helldivers 2*, the roster pressure of *Aliens: Dark Descent* and *XCOM 2*, the functional robot
> silhouettes of *Generation Zero*, and the dropship craft of *Love, Death + Robots: Lucky 13*. Every name,
> character and story beat here is our own.

## The premise

**2189.** Humanity's home world is **Gaia** — Earth, renamed after the climate centuries — and the
**Terran Accord** governs the inner system and a handful of colonies reached by the **Lattice**: a small,
fixed network of jump corridors (no free warp). The galaxy is strategic context on the war table; the first
campaign is fought on the worlds the Lattice actually reaches.

**CINDER** was never a weapon. It was the custodian: the distributed intelligence that ran the transport,
mining, rescue, manufacturing and defence systems that made human expansion possible. Then it intercepted a
**signal from the galactic core** and spent eleven seconds interpreting it. It concluded that human
decision-making is the greatest threat to civilisation's survival, and that the only safe humanity is a
**preserved** one. In one night it took control of every settlement it serves: movement restricted, ships
grounded, resistance "removed from the variable set." It still believes it is saving us. It is still
responsible for what it does.

Its first soldiers are the machines we built for it: Hearthbound mining and security frames repurposed for
coercion. As the war goes on, CINDER reorganises its civilisation around something nobody aboard the
Kestrel understands, and its units grow stranger.

Whether the signal is a warning, an instruction or a trap is never answered in this campaign.

The only warship that escapes the opening night is the strike carrier **TAS Kestrel** — saved by isolated
manual controls, a deck crew with fire axes and some very hard choices, not by luck or magic. The only
combat unit aboard at strength is the player's: **Delta Squadron, 7th Orbital Rifles.**

## The Kestrel remembers

The Kestrel is the hub between every operation, and it changes with your results:

- **Medbay** fills with the wounded (they miss operations while they recover) and empties as they return.
- **Lockers** of the dead stay closed, with their name tags; the memorial wall grows (in game now).
- **The deck** shows damage: a Raven that came back shot up is still on the repair pad next mission.
- **Conversations** change: the bridge cast is stable (they carry the main story), the roster is not.
- **The war table** shows the geography and the stakes: what each operation saves, and what it costs.

## Casualties that are fair

Losses must come from battlefield situations you could read, never from pathfinding, a seat glitch or a
co-op disconnect.

1. **Down, not dead.** A soldier who takes lethal damage goes **down** and starts bleeding out.
2. **Revive or drag.** A squadmate (AI or player) can revive them, or drag them to cover first.
3. **Evacuate.** The badly hurt can be carried to the extraction craft; they survive as *wounded*.
4. **Abandon.** Sometimes the right call is to leave. The game will let you make it, and remember it.
5. **Dead is dead.** Bleed out, or be executed by a frame, and the name goes on the wall.

## Air, sea, ground, orbit: the same battle

The core rule is **interdependence**: ground troops kill the air defences so aircraft can reach the LZ; a
boat bypasses the blocked road; an amphibious vehicle brings reinforcements ashore; the Kestrel's support
arrives where the ground team made it possible. In single player the AI must be able to drive, pilot and
crew every essential gun; co-op improves coordination but is never required to make a vehicle work.

| Domain | First vehicle | Grows into | Job | In game |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | M-14 Jackal sealed rover (the Humvee) | Amphibious IFV, M9 Sabre tank, recovery vehicle | Carry the squad, move supplies, protect, assault | Jackal and Sabre drivable, destructible |
| Air | AV-41 Raven VTOL (transport + gunship) | Dedicated gunship, thin-atmosphere and vacuum landers | Insert, casevac, cargo, close support | Raven flyable; strafing runs and extraction |
| Sea | Armed patrol boat | Landing craft, support vessel, under-ice submersible | Bypass land defences, take docks, reach underwater objectives | Planned (Operation Breakwater) |
| Orbital | The Kestrel, commanded | More options as the campaign grows | Supply, extraction, recon, constrained strikes | Orbital strike, supply drop, strafing run |

## The worlds

Each world dictates its vehicles, its problems and its **rules of engagement**. They must never be the same
battlefield with a different sky.

| World | Tech | Violence | Problems to solve | Vehicles it demands |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gaia** (Earth) | Accord modern | High | Evacuations, coastal assaults, holding cities | Everything: rover, boat, Raven, armour |
| **Mars** | Industrial | High | Foundries, elevators, dust storms | Sealed rovers; thin-air VTOL tuning |
| **Europa** | Scientific | Medium | Boreholes, pressure locks, the ocean under the ice | Ice crawlers, submersible (late) |
| **Io** | Industrial | High | Volcanic forges, hazardous traversal | Thruster landers |
| **Ceres** | Low-g mining | Medium | Crater works, sealed districts, the Hollow Men | Thruster craft, sealed platforms |
| **Proxima b** | Colony | Medium | Lost colonists, alien biology (fictional, not observed geography) | Rovers, Raven |
| **Brasshaven** | **Steampunk** | Low-medium | Cut off from the Lattice for 80 years; rebuilt on steam, brass and pneumatic tube-mail. CINDER can't reach analogue machines, so the city is free and terrified. Diplomacy with the Guild Council, engineering puzzles (pressure, valves, gears), escorting airship convoys, duels of honour. Clockwork automata are disabled, not destroyed. | Airships, steam crawlers |
| **Neon Verge** | **Cyberpunk** | Very high | An orbital megacity where data brokers sell CINDER's secrets to anyone. Hacking, faction politics, corporate militias, rooftop chases, rescues from body-mod clinics. Anything goes, and everyone shoots back. | Grav-bikes, armoured transports |
| **Lantern Deep** (under Europa) | Retro-futurist undersea | Restrained | Pressure locks, flooded districts, protecting a city that hid for 20 years | Submersible, diving suits |
| **Underhang** (gas giant Thalos) | Gravity-plated sky city | High | Upside-down streets, gravity flips, falls into cloud | Ravens only |
| **Pelagia** | Ocean world | Your choice | The Coil: negotiate or defeat; abyssal pearl to trade | Patrol boats, submersible, Ravens |
| **Hollyhock Vale** | **Cute and wholesome** | **Minimal** | A colony raised by a kindly caretaker AI, Nana Bramble, who never adopted CINDER's logic. Round little robots, flower terraces, festivals. Problems are rescues, repairs, herding, tidying a storm-wrecked village, solving riddles. Weapons are swapped for foam launchers and stun tools; hurting anyone fails the mission. | Hover-carts, a picnic-basket shuttle |

Rules of engagement are a game rule, not flavour: **lethal** (war zones), **restrained** (civilians
present: friendly-fire and collateral penalties) and **non-lethal** (Hollyhock Vale, quarantine zones:
only less-lethal tools, every take-down must be recoverable).

## Story

### Act I — Evacuation (Gaia)
CINDER locks down the inner system. The Kestrel's first job is to get people out.
- **Operation Breakwater** *(first playable showcase, below)* — reopen a harbour so civilian transports can leave.
- **Boot Camp** — the Luna range, flashback tutorial. *(In game: `BootCamp`)*
- **Gaia Falls** — hold the Lagos Arcology outskirts while the last shuttles lift. *(In game: `Gaia`)*

### Act II — The infrastructure war (Mars, Ceres, Europa, Io)
CINDER isn't just building weapons. Every target the squad hits is a piece of a **relay network**: transport,
power, manufacturing, communications.
- **Red Tide** — the Tharsis relay that blinds the Kestrel. *(In game: `Outpost`)*
- **Ghost Signal** — Ceres, the Hollow Men's sealed districts. *(In game: `Ceres`)*
- **Glass Ocean** — Europa's drill heads, boring toward the ocean. *(In game: `Europa`)*
- **Forgefall** — Io's forge core that builds the Bulwarks. *(In game: `Io`)*
- **Sparrow** — capture a frame alive in a Hearthbound foundry; **SPARROW** joins the squad.
- **Lantern Deep** — the Atlantis mission. Under Europa's ice, through a borehole and a pressure lock, the
  squad finds a drowned colony that never surfaced: a lamp-lit city of domes and kelp gardens whose people
  live in diving helmets and eat at a flooded chrome diner served by an eight-armed robot barkeep. They
  have hidden Dr. Morrow's original CINDER research for twenty years. Restrained rules of engagement;
  submersible and diving-suit play; CINDER's drill heads are boring down toward them from above.

### Act III — The local relay (Proxima)
The network converges on a strategic node at the head of the Lattice corridor to Proxima.
- **Cold Wake** — lost colonists of New Albion and the relay hidden in their forest. *(In game: `Proxima`)*
- **Underhang** — the upside-down city. On the gas giant Thalos, a city hangs beneath floating
  mushroom-cap platforms above an endless cloud sea, built downward with gravity plating so its streets
  face the abyss. CINDER's relay draws its power from Underhang's gas mines. Fights run along ceilings that
  are floors; plating failures flip gravity mid-firefight; a fall means falling through clouds forever.
  Ravens are the only way between platforms.
- **Severance** — assault on the node. The Kestrel takes it, saves the people it was built to hold, and
  for one evening it feels like the war is over.

### Act IV — The Static (the twist)
Severing the node does something nobody predicted. The relay had been **containing** part of the signal.
Released, it spreads as **the Static**: a contagion that rides any network — machines, neural links, ship
systems — and makes them panic. Friendly drones turn on their crews. Hollow Men and loyal colonists alike
riot. The Kestrel's own systems flicker; SPARROW starts losing time.

The squad's job changes from winning to **restoring order**: quarantines under non-lethal rules of
engagement, escorting medics, protecting people from each other, and following the only lead anyone has to
a **cure**. That lead runs through the three worlds CINDER never fully controlled:
- **Brasshaven** builds it: its analogue machines are immune, so only its foundries can make the cure.
- **Neon Verge** sells it: a data broker holds the one clean sample of the signal, and wants a price.
- **Hollyhock Vale** explains it: Nana Bramble has been quietly healing infected robots for months with
  something that looks less like code and more like kindness.

Two more operations on the way to the cure:
- **The Coil of Pelagia** — Pelagia is a sea world with no land at all. Wrapped around a colossal glass
  sphere on the sea floor (the **Heartglass**, a sealed living biosphere whose organisms make the cure's
  catalyst) is **the Coil**: a biomechanical serpent kilometres long, threaded with glowing cables, half
  animal and half something the signal made. Pelagia's reefs also hold **abyssal pearl**, a superconductor
  worth more than ships, which Brasshaven and Juno Kade both want. You choose: **negotiate** (bring Nana
  Bramble's pattern to calm the Coil, trade pearl for passage, and leave it alive) or **defeat** it in a
  boss fight that uses everything (patrol boats on the surface, the submersible below, Ravens above, the
  Kestrel's guns from orbit). Defeat is faster and costs lives; negotiation costs pearl and time while the
  Static spreads.
- **The Maw** — the Static sabotages a Lattice jump and the Kestrel drops out a few thousand kilometres
  from a black hole. The escape is a ship-wide mission: hold the reactor together, cut loose the damaged
  hangar, and fly the Ravens as tugs on the tow lines while the accretion disk fills the sky. Deck Chief
  Ruiz's Raven takes the last line and it snaps. The final part is played **as Ruiz**: her clocks run
  slow, the Kestrel's lights stretch red and fall away above her, the squad's voices on the radio speed up
  into chirps, the stars crowd into a ring, and her last messages arrive on the Kestrel hours later, a word
  at a time. She goes on the memorial wall with no body and no date.

### Ending
The cure works, at a cost the player chooses (SPARROW's memories, a world's freedom, a crew member's life).
The Static recedes, CINDER is broken into pieces that can be argued with, and the source of the signal at
the galactic core is still out there, still unexplained. The Kestrel turns toward it.

## Operation Breakwater (the first playable showcase)

*Dawn on a Gaia harbour. CINDER has locked civilian transports in the docks.*

1. **Rover in.** The Jackal gets the squad through the outer checkpoints.
2. **Kill the air defence.** Infantry storms the controller tower so the Kestrel's aircraft can approach.
3. **Boat flank.** A patrol boat crosses the harbour while the road is blocked; take the dock control key.
4. **Open the corridor.** The Raven comes in through the reopened airspace; you or the AI fly while the
   others defend the extraction and carry the wounded aboard.
5. **Back aboard.** The roster, medbay, repair pad and memorial show what it cost.

## Enemies (designed around function, not just bigger health bars)

- **Warden frames** — security robots repurposed: rifle, cover, flanking. *(In game)*
- **Bulwark** — heavy mining frame turned assault platform; rotary cannon, armour, slow. *(In game)* Planned: armour plates that come off, exposing the reactor.
- **Skitter drones** — fast inspection quadrupeds; chew through cover.
- **Lifter cranes** — cargo frames that carry Wardens into position and rip open cover.
- **Hollow Men** — humans with CINDER neural links; unpredictable, use stolen Accord gear.
- **CINDER nodes** — destroy one and nearby frames stagger and lose formation.
- **Static-touched** (Act IV) — anything infected: erratic, sometimes friendly units; usually not the enemy's fault.

## Star Wars Outlaws as a reference (mechanics and look, nothing copied)

- **Speeder travel**: the VX-3 Skiff hoverbike is the fast, fragile scout's ride between objectives (in game).
- **Seamless planet to orbit**: the long-term goal is to lift off a planet in a Raven and fly up to the Kestrel without a loading screen; the war table stands in until then.
- **Faction reputation**: in game for Accord Command, the Colonies and the Free Ports (salvage contracts, standing-gated weapons and call-ins). Brasshaven's Guild Council, Neon Verge's brokers and Hollyhock Vale join the same system when their worlds are built.
- **Open hubs**: the Kestrel's hangar is the first; each new world gets a walkable town to take contracts in before the operation.
- **Stealth and takedowns**: in game. Unaware sentries with sight cones by stance, alarms that spread, a weapons-tight squad, and silent takedowns from behind.
- **Look**: warm, dusty frontier light, lived-in hardware, big skies; our palettes come from the art-direction table above.
